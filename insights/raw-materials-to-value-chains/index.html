From Raw Materials to Greater Value: Rethinking Africa's Place in Global Supply Chains

Exporting unrefined commodities leaves the overwhelming majority of wealth abroad. How structured offtake, local refining, and ERP traceability change the paradigm.

✦ Au Doré Traceability ✦ In-Country Value Retention ✦ Odoo ERP Provenance
Joseph Fabian Ayitevi Managing Director, AFA-EF Mining Ltd
· 7 min read · Draft Topic
Executive Briefing At A Glance
  • Beyond Raw Extraction: Retaining in-country economic value requires capturing the 80%+ spread currently lost to foreign smelting, assaying, and vaulting.
  • Digital Provenance Lineage: Integrating Odoo ERP traceability from pit to export vault guarantees ethical compliance and eliminates illicit intermediaries.
  • Bilateral Offtake Structure: AFA-EF Mining's agreements with The Bullion Mart (Toronto) prove that formalized local supply commands premium North American pricing.

For over a century, the economic paradigm of resource-rich African nations has been defined by extraction: dig up ore, harvest cocoa beans, drill crude, and ship them immediately to foreign shores. The refining, branding, financial structuring, and secondary manufacturing—where over 80% of economic value is captured—take place elsewhere.

In the gold sector, Ghana has demonstrated remarkable progress through the Ghana Gold Board's formalized aggregation and compliance frameworks. Our agreement at AFA-EF Mining with The Bullion Mart Corporation of Toronto represents an intentional step toward higher-tier integration.

"National economic sovereignty cannot be achieved through raw extraction alone. Digitally certified local value addition is the only path to sustainable prosperity."

Joseph Fabian Ayitevi · MD, AFA-EF Mining Ltd

By integrating digital ERP traceability from the pit through assaying to bullion offtake, we establish high-grade Au doré standards that command institutional pricing and direct access to North American bullion markets.

The next frontier is expanding domestic refining and accredited vaulting capacity across West Africa. When an African producer can deliver hallmarked bullion bars that meet London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) standards, the cost of trade finance falls and domestic capital reserves swell.

Industrial policy must incentivize processing equipment, dependable electricity, and technical metallurgical training. Moving up the value chain is not a luxury; it is an economic imperative.
