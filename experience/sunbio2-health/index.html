Case Study 03 · Healthcare Infrastructure

Clinical Oxygen Infrastructure & Sustainable Energy Deployments

Eliminating clinical oxygen deficits across regional Ghanaian hospitals through decentralized Pressure Swing Adsorption generation systems backed by clean microgrid power.

✦ Decentralized PSA Generation ✦ Ministry of Health Compliance ✦ Solar PV Microgrids

The Operational Context

Medical oxygen is recognized by the WHO as an indispensable clinical medicine. However, regional and district healthcare facilities across Africa frequently suffer acute oxygen shortages due to fragile distribution logistics, exorbitant cylinder transportation costs, and erratic grid electricity.

The Strategic Challenge

Overcoming reliance on imported pressurized cylinders by engineering self-sustaining, on-site hospital oxygen generation infrastructure that operates reliably despite grid load-shedding and high ambient humidity.

Role & Executed Actions

Role: Chief Executive Officer, Sunbio2 Ghana Limited (Dec 2021–Present).

  • Managed procurement, importation, engineering installation, and commissioning of Pressure Swing Adsorption (PSA) oxygen generator plants.
  • Ensured full compliance with medical device regulatory frameworks and Ministry of Health purity standards (93% ± 3% medical oxygen).
  • Coupled generation plants with renewable solar microgrid backups to maintain continuous life-support operation during power cuts.
  • Trained hospital technical staff in preventative maintenance and cylinder manifold operations.

Documented Outcomes

  • Secured uninterrupted, hospital-grade oxygen supply for critical care and maternity wards.
  • Significantly reduced per-liter oxygen costs compared to commercial cylinder haulage.
  • Created a replicable model joining clean energy with essential health infrastructure.

Core Executive Lesson

Clinical infrastructure succeeds only when coupled with local preventative maintenance training and reliable decentralized power. Medical technology and energy resilience are inseparable.
